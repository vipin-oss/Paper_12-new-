\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%% ===========================================================================

A two-dimensional $C^1$ Bogner--Fox--Schmit finite element was developed for
piezoelectric--flexoelectric plates. The strain-gradient energy is generated by
the second-moment tensor $M$ of a rectangular averaging window, the electric
enthalpy carries the piezoelectric and the flexoelectric couplings, and the
dielectric memory is a fractional Kelvin--Voigt law of Caputo order $\alpha$,
discretized by the L1 scheme on reduced modal coordinates.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The window enters only through $M$ ($a_i^{2}/3$, $a_i^{2}/4$,
  $a_i^{2}/5$ for cuboid, ellipse, ellipsoid): equal-$M$ windows give identical
  solutions; anisotropy changes the coupling by $15$--$19\,\%$ and the frequency
  by $6$--$8\,\%$.
\item For the \SI{200}{\micro\metre}$\times$\SI{10}{\micro\metre} plate
  $k^{2}=0.5581$, within $0.5\,\%$ of $0.5608$ and constant to
  $0.09\,\%$ from $10$ to \SI{1}{\micro\metre}.
\item The flexoelectric bending coupling rises $24$-fold ($31$-fold with the
  piezoelectric terms off) from $10$ to \SI{1}{\micro\metre}, slope $-1.96$ to
  $-0.43$; at \SI{1}{\micro\metre} it is \num{4.2e-3}, two orders below the
  extensional one.
\item The configurations are not additive: the bending piezoelectric charge
  vanishes ($k^{2}\sim10^{-10}$), the extensional gives $58$ times the
  open-circuit voltage.
\item Short-circuit memory damps $0.449$ times condensed memory; the
  integer-order limit is reproduced to $0.15\,\%$.
\item At $\omega\tau=0.3$ the integer-order element over-predicts the modulus
  by $3.5\,\%$ ($\alpha=1$) and $22.6\,\%$ ($\alpha=0.7$); fractional poles fan
  upward, integer-order poles over-damp.
\end{enumerate}

The framework targets thin piezoelectric--flexoelectric sensing
plates: polarization and electrode arrangement, the thickness range in which
the flexoelectric term matters, the window orientation and the damping at the
operating point, provided $\alpha$, $\tau$ and the memory matrix are stated.
Absolute values require a measured $\mu$ and $\rho$, assumed here; three
dimensions and a thermal coupling of the memory are the next step.
