\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%% ===========================================================================

A two-dimensional $C^1$-continuous finite element framework was developed for
piezoelectric--flexoelectric plates that carry a rectangular nonlocal averaging
window, a polarization-gradient coupling and a fractional dielectric memory.
The Bogner--Fox--Schmit interpolation carries the second displacement gradient
as a nodal quantity, the strain-gradient energy is generated by the in-plane
second-moment tensor of the window, and the memory is a fractional
Kelvin--Voigt law of Caputo order $\alpha$ discretised by the L1 scheme on
reduced modal coordinates. The formulation was verified against analytical
gradient solutions, against the classical limits of every switch it contains,
and against closed-form coupling factors obtained from the constitutive
tensors alone; the thin-plate limit was additionally shown to be reproducible
to two significant digits across eigensolver paths and numeric stacks, and that
statement is quantified rather than assumed.

The principal findings of the study are summarised as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item A geometric statement replaces a fitting parameter: the averaging window
  enters the model only through its second-moment tensor, with
  $M_{ii}=a_i^{2}/3$, $a_i^{2}/4$ and $a_i^{2}/5$ for cuboid, ellipse and
  ellipsoid alike. Windows of equal second moment produce identical solutions
  to $10^{-3}$, so the shape of the window is not a parameter once its second
  moment is fixed, whereas the anisotropy and the orientation of the window
  change the extensional coupling by 15--19\,\% over the range examined.
\item The two memory matrices that a condensed electrical problem admits are
  different models, not two conventions. Memory carried on the short-circuit
  stiffness gives $0.449$ times the damping of memory carried on the condensed
  stiffness at $\alpha=1$, the integer-order limit is reproduced to
  $0.15\,\%$, and the ratio does not reduce to the naive $1-k^{2}$ estimate.
\item A fractional element is not a cosmetic refinement of a
  single-relaxation fit: at $\omega\tau=0.3$ the integer-order Kelvin--Voigt
  element over-predicts the modulus by $3.5\,\%$ at $\alpha=1$ and by
  $22.6\,\%$ at $\alpha=0.7$, and at $\omega\tau=100$ it is wrong by a factor
  $91$. The pole, not the modulus, is what a design should be read from, and
  fractional poles fan upward in the complex plane where the integer-order ones
  over-damp.
\item For the \SI{200}{\micro\metre} by \SI{10}{\micro\metre} plate the
  extensional coupling factor is $k^{2}=0.5581$, within $0.5\,\%$ of the value
  that the constitutive tensors give on their own, and it changes by
  $0.09\,\%$ between \SI{10}{\micro\metre} and \SI{1}{\micro\metre}. The
  flexoelectric bending coupling of the same plate rises by a factor $24$ over
  that range, or $31$ with the piezoelectric terms removed. Thinning the plate
  is therefore free of penalty for the piezoelectric response and strongly
  favourable for the flexoelectric one.
\item The two configurations are two electrical problems on one geometry and
  their coupling factors do not add: on the bending configuration the
  piezoelectric charge of the mode integrates to zero and the piezoelectric-only
  coupling is $1.0\times10^{-10}$, while the extensional configuration gives a
  factor $58$ more voltage at equal amplitude. A design map that reports a
  single coupling number for both is therefore under-specified in the same way
  as one that reports a single damping number.
\end{itemize}

From an engineering standpoint the framework locates the thickness, the window
orientation and the operating band in which the flexoelectric term dominates
the piezoelectric one in a bending device, together with the damping that the
dielectric loss of the same material imposes at that operating point. It does
not show that a flexoelectric device outperforms an extensional piezoelectric
one: at \SI{1}{\micro\metre} the flexoelectric bending coupling
$4.2\times10^{-3}$ is two orders of magnitude below the extensional coupling
$0.5581$ of the same plate, and the open-circuit voltage of the extensional
configuration is larger by a factor $58$ at equal tip amplitude. What thinning
buys is a configuration in which the piezoelectric charge integrates to zero
and the flexoelectric term is the only one that survives; the price is the two
orders of magnitude. Two parameters are assumed rather than measured
throughout, the flexoelectric coefficient
$\mu=10^{-6}$\,\si{\coulomb\per\metre} and the density $\rho$ of the single
crystal, and every flexoelectric number in this paper scales as $\mu^{2}$ and
as $\rho$. Future work will substitute measured values for both, extend the
formulation to three dimensions and to ferroelectric hysteresis, and couple the
fractional memory to a thermal field so that the relaxation time follows the
temperature of the device.
