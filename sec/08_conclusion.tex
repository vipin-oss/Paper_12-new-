\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%% ===========================================================================

A two-dimensional $C^1$-continuous finite element was developed for
piezoelectric--flexoelectric plates that carry a rectangular nonlocal averaging
window, a polarization-gradient coupling and a fractional dielectric memory;
the Bogner--Fox--Schmit interpolation makes the second displacement gradient a
nodal quantity and the L1 scheme carries the Caputo memory on reduced modal
coordinates. The window enters only through its second-moment tensor, with
$M_{ii}=a_i^{2}/3$, $a_i^{2}/4$ and $a_i^{2}/5$ for cuboid, ellipse and
ellipsoid alike, so windows of equal $M$ give identical solutions while their
anisotropy and orientation change the extensional coupling by
$15$--$19\,\%$. Memory on the short-circuit stiffness damps $0.449$ times as
much as memory on the condensed one, and the integer-order limit is reproduced
to $0.15\,\%$. For the \SI{200}{\micro\metre} by \SI{10}{\micro\metre} plate
$k^{2}=0.5581$, within $0.5\,\%$ of the constitutive value and constant to
$0.09\,\%$ between \SI{10}{\micro\metre} and \SI{1}{\micro\metre}, while the
flexoelectric bending coupling rises by a factor $24$ over the same range. The
two configurations remain two electrical problems whose coupling factors do not
add.

Two limitations bound these statements. The flexoelectric coefficient
$\mu=10^{-6}$\,\si{\coulomb\per\metre} and the density $\rho$ are assumed
rather than measured, and every flexoelectric number scales as $\mu^{2}$ and as
$\rho$. At \SI{1}{\micro\metre} the flexoelectric bending coupling
$4.2\times10^{-3}$ is still two orders of magnitude below the extensional
coupling of the same plate, so thinning buys a configuration in which the
flexoelectric term is the only one that survives, not a larger coupling.
Measured parameters, three dimensions and a thermal coupling of the memory are
the next steps.
